## Fact-check notes

### PolyUMI and VisTA

- The paper reports 20 fps tactile video at 1152 by 648, 16 kHz mono contact audio and 60 fps wrist video at 1920 by 1080. Policy inputs are aligned at 10 Hz.
- The transferable sensing finger is costed at $235.96 and four assembly hours; transfer between collector and robot takes about ten minutes.
- Slip control uses ten trials per sensor condition: vision-touch-audio is 8/10 and vision alone 2/10. Board wiping reaches 75% full and 100% full-plus-partial success; lightbulb turning is at least 80% for every method and vision-only performs best.
- The repository is MIT-licensed, but its anonymous review copy says private forks remain unresolved placeholders. It was tested on a 48 GB RTX 6000 Ada and estimates at least 32 GB.
- The current repository README says tactile video is 10 fps at 540 by 480, unlike the paper and project page. The article reports this discrepancy rather than choosing an undocumented explanation.

### PROPRA

- The AIST/CNRS-AIST gripper has three pressure-sensitive tactile and six reflective proximity channels. Each sensor history covers 0.5 seconds.
- All policies use 20 demonstrations per task. Results are means over three seeds and 20 trials per seed on PickCup, PickSponge, MovePen and OpenLid.
- PROPRA averages 75.0%, versus 71.7% for image anchoring and 72.1% for image anchoring plus direct pairing. It is lower than image anchoring on OpenLid, 73.3% versus 76.7%.
- Pretraining and policy training take about three to four and eight hours respectively on one 141 GB NVIDIA H200. This is a reported setup, not a minimum specification.
- The official page says code is coming soon. No repository, weights, dataset or software license was verified.

### Whole-hand force regulation

- The controller samples about 3,000 candidate points over all hand links, performs signed-distance queries in under 1 ms and solves its chosen five-candidate-per-link QP at about 84 Hz.
- Simulation disables gravity. It uses 790 power grasps over 79 YCB objects; the dynamic-perturbation comparison uses five representative objects and 50 grasps per method.
- At 90 degrees, whole-hand succeeds at 88.6% versus 72.4% for fingertip-only. Under constant 30-degree and 5 mm pose offsets, adaptive control reaches 87% to 93%; the test does not cover latency or time-varying tracking failure.
- Hardware is a 27-DoF Flexiv/Robotis arm-hand with one RealSense. Three uncut runs contain 78 manual perturbations and eight losses; six losses are recovered.
- The project says code is coming soon. No repository or software license was verified.

### Res-HIL

- Res-HIL freezes a 20-demonstration ACT policy and trains a zero-initialized TD3 residual at 7 Hz. Interventions provide residual behavior-cloning targets and reward shaping.
- Five conditions cover two peg-in-hole tasks, vent insertion and two cable-camera variants. Every evaluation uses 50 fixed randomized starts, and online experiments are repeated three times.
- Ten-minute success is 100%, 64%, 50%, 66% and 92%. Final success is 100%, 96%, 100%, 88% and 100%.
- HIL-SERL is faster on insertion tasks it solves but scores 0% on both cable settings. The paper excludes two concurrent residual-HIL methods because controlled real-robot reproduction was unavailable before submission.
- One operator supplies all interventions. No public code, checkpoints, training data or software license was verified.
