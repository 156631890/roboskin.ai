## Fact-check notes

### Better Curriculum

- Main ACT comparison: 19/20 stable grasps from reflex-shaped data versus 3/20 from visually screened manual data. Contact-screened manual data reached 6/20 in one run.
- pi0.5 nominal comparison: 19/20 versus 1/20. Paper-cup OOD comparison was 8/10 versus 3/10 with `p=0.0698`, treated as exploratory.
- Disturbance test uses the same reflex-data pi0.5 policy: 11/20 retained grasps without the arbiter and 20/20 with it.
- The controller force is a dimensionless vision-tactile proxy, not Newtons. Outcome labels come from video ratings. Collection order was not randomized.
- The official page linked the paper and an author's general GitHub profile, but no dedicated code, dataset, checkpoint or asset license.

### VisForce

- Hardware: UR10, Inspire RH56F1 and two RealSense D405 cameras. Per-finger actuator forces are not direct contact-force vectors.
- Demonstrations per task: 30, 25, 30 and 30. Every reported task-policy evaluation uses 20 trials.
- Final success: 14/20, 11/20 and 8/20 on the three multi-stage tasks. The base pi0.5 results are 4/20, 1/20 and 1/20.
- Removing cross-attention while retaining visual cues yields 2/20, 1/20 and 3/20.
- No public official code, data, checkpoint or project page was linked from the paper record or full text.

### CableVLA

- Main benchmark: 345 MuJoCo rollouts, formed from 115 scenario-condition instances repeated three times. CableVLA reports 84.9%, pi0.5-V 62.6% and the topology-only parent 65.2%.
- Each Loong96 array has 96 taxels; TacSense uses 25 frames at 30 Hz. The 0.049 N threshold gates residual control and is not the sensor's complete range.
- Internal assets include 33,654 topology windows from 1,228 training episodes, 7,338 tactile episodes over 57 tasks and 2,004 policy-adaptation demonstrations.
- Slip AUPRC scores are simulated representation tests, not physical routing success.
- Isaac Sim transfer is 27/50 versus 17/50; real zero-shot transfer is only 4/10 versus 1/10. No public code/data release was linked.

### Cartesian Hand

- Seven prismatic axes: four fingertip slides, two gripper openings and inter-gripper separation. Reported mass is 850 g and total estimated cost is about USD 500.
- 35 configured objects times 10 trials equals 350 successful trials. Initial positions are prescribed and new objects can require up to five setup trials.
- Humanoid use is qualitative and does not repeat the 350-trial arm evaluation.
- The hand does not observe object pose, contact location or contact force. Theoretical rack force is not measured continuous fingertip force.
- The project page links `generalroboticslab/Cartesian_Hand`, but that repository returned 404 on September 23 through both the public page and GitHub API. The paper's future open-source statement is not recorded as a current release.
