## NUS underwater self-healing sensor

Primary sources read: https://cde.nus.edu.sg/news/electronic-skin-that-senses-and-self-heals-under-water/ (July 17, 2026 university report) and https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/adma.202523052 (first published April 18, 2026). Main-text Sections 2.1–2.5 and 4.1–4.5 are readable; no share-access terms accepted, no supplemental payload or experiment run.

The useful new distinction is what healed: electrical continuity, material elongation recovery and calibrated task performance require different evidence. Section 4.4 defines mechanical healing efficiency as healed elongation divided by initial elongation; it is not the percentage of robots repaired or success across damage cases. Early electrical restoration did not withstand the same cyclic large deformations as longer healing (2.3). Severe damage in the robot-hand example required intervention or replacement (2.5), so autonomous repair is bounded. Proximity setup uses relative motion of magnetic components (2.4), not universal detection of arbitrary nonmagnetic obstacles. Self-powered transduction is not evidence that signal processing, Bluetooth, LEDs or the robot need no energy. Keep April paper date and July institutional report distinct. Do not infer deployment readiness, medical benefit, subsea communication range, depth rating or safety certification.
